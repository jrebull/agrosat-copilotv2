\section{Phenology-Contrastive FarSLIP: From Aligning Against Noise to a
Vision-Language Signal}
\label{sec:farslip_method}

This section documents the methodological path that lifts FarSLIP from a negative
ablation --- it lost $0.163$ macro-F1 against AlphaEarth's $0.233$ over the 18
PASTIS-R classes --- to a vision-language component whose contribution is
\textit{complementary and honest}. The account is deliberately transparent: we
report the bug that caused the negative result, the fix that closes FarSLIP's
\textit{own} gap, and the fact that, even once corrected, the faithful FarSLIP
space \textbf{does not beat} AlphaEarth as a dense classifier. Its value lies in
the heterogeneous ensemble and in multi-region transferability
(Sec.~\ref{sec:farslip_transfer}).

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F1 --- Bug Diagnosis: Contrastive Alignment Against Noise}
\label{sec:farslip_f1}

FarSLIP's region-category alignment loss~\cite{li2025farslip}
(\texttt{RegionCategoryAlignmentLoss}, an InfoNCE with temperature $0.07$) aligns
the student's \texttt{[CLS]} token against a matrix of \textit{text prototypes}.
In the original implementation (\texttt{ml/farslip/train.py}, path
\texttt{proto\_source="random"}) that matrix was initialized with
\texttt{torch.randn(n\_protos, hidden\_dim)}: Gaussian noise with no semantic
content. The contrast pushed the \texttt{[CLS]} token toward random vectors, so
the categorical signal was never anchored to a real crop description. The
docstring of \texttt{build\_text\_prototypes} names the symptom verbatim:
\textit{``contrastive alignment against noise --- the bug that made FarSLIP lose
0.163 vs 0.233''}. This is the origin of the negative result: not a failure of
the foundation model, but an initialization that turned a contrastive loss into
regularization toward noise.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{F2 --- Real Phenological Prototypes and Frozen Orthogonal Reprojection}
\label{sec:farslip_f2}

The fix replaces the noise with \textit{real phenological prototypes}, one class
at a time. For each of the 18 PASTIS-R classes we write a phenological
description following the framework of Wen et al.~\cite{wen2025phenology}
(greenup onset, NDVI peak, senescence date, canopy texture), generated with
Gemini~Flash and encoded with MiniLM (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}) into an
L2-normalized 384-dimensional embedding. The flow (path
\texttt{proto\_source="pastis"}) is:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \texttt{load\_class\_prototype\_embeddings()} $\rightarrow$
    \texttt{proto\_18} $(18, 384)$, per-class MiniLM prototypes.
    \item \texttt{expand\_to\_cap()} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{proto\_cap}
    $(32, 384)$, mapping the PASTIS-18 scheme onto the 32-category CAP
    vocabulary.
    \item \texttt{np.tile()} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{proto\_tiled}
    $(n\_regions \cdot 32, 384)$ in \textit{region-major} order
    (\texttt{region $\cdot$ n\_categories + category}), matching the loss
    target.
\end{enumerate}

The 384-dimensional \textit{tile} is reprojected to the student's
\texttt{[CLS]} dimension via a semi-orthogonal matrix $W$ generated with
\texttt{torch.nn.init.orthogonal\_}, \texttt{requires\_grad=False}: it never
enters the optimizer or the \texttt{state\_dict}. Being orthogonal, it preserves
the $L_2$ norm and the angles of the MiniLM subspace, so the 18 classes remain
distinguishable after reprojection.

\paragraph{Factual correction on dimensionality.}
The reprojection of the \textit{loss} prototypes is \textbf{$384\rightarrow768$},
not $384\rightarrow512$: the \texttt{hidden\_size} of the teacher CLIP ViT-B/16
is $768$, confirmed by \texttt{trainer.teacher.config.hidden\_size} and by the
\texttt{n\_dims}$=768$ column of the evaluation artefact. The ``512-dim per
parcel'' figure corresponds to a different object --- the \textit{feature} bank
that the extractor produces at parcel level --- and must not be confused with the
InfoNCE prototype reprojection. We distinguish them explicitly: parcel-bank
$512$-dim versus prototypes/\texttt{[CLS]} $768$-dim.

\paragraph{Effect of the fix on the own gap.}
The fix closes FarSLIP's \textit{own} gap: the faithful v2 space goes from
macro-F1 $0.163$ (against noise) to $0.5551 \pm 0.0205$ (with phenological
prototypes) over the same 567 parcels, an improvement of $+0.392$ over the buggy
baseline. Table~\ref{tab:farslip_vs_alphaearth} reports the honest comparison
against AlphaEarth.

\input{../tables/farslip-method/farslip_vs_alphaearth.tex}

\paragraph{Defensible claim.}
With the bug fixed, the faithful FarSLIP space \textbf{is still below} AlphaEarth
($-0.0024$ in macro-F1, $-0.0025$ in silhouette). We do not claim that FarSLIP
beats AlphaEarth or Gemini: FarSLIP's contribution is as a complementary ensemble
member and in multi-region transfer, not as a winning dense classifier. This is
the reading the rest of the section supports.

\subsection{F3 --- 3:1 Meadow Per-Parcel Filter (\texttt{dominance\_ratio})}
\label{sec:farslip_f3}

PASTIS-R is dominated by \textit{Meadow} (class 1) and \textit{Background}
(class 0), which together can cover $>80\%$ of a patch. Extracting
\textit{features} over such patches contaminates the embedding space with
low-information signal. The previous filter operated in \texttt{coverage} mode
(legacy): it kept a patch if the combined coverage of the target classes over the
valid pixels exceeded a global threshold. The present work introduces the
\texttt{dominance\_ratio} mode in \texttt{ml/data/pastis\_filter.py}: it keeps a
patch if $\text{Meadow}_{px} \leq 3 \cdot \text{second}_{px}$, where
$\text{second}_{px}$ is the largest count \textit{within that same patch} among
the target classes, excluding Meadow, Background~(0) and Void~(19) by literal
identifier (not via \texttt{ignore\_index}) so as not to contaminate the ratio.
The comparison is inclusive ($\leq$): a patch that is exactly 3:1 is kept.

The difference from the \texttt{coverage} filter is conceptual: \texttt{coverage}
measures \textit{global} purity of target classes; \texttt{dominance\_ratio}
bounds the \textit{local} Meadow imbalance relative to the second class of each
patch, discarding only patches over-dominated by meadow without throwing away the
minority signal of the rest. Explicit, tested edge cases:
$\text{Meadow}_{px}=0$ is kept (metric $0.0$); $\text{Meadow}_{px}>0$ with
$\text{second}_{px}=0$ (no competing class) is discarded (metric $\infty$).

\subsection{F4 --- Band Ablation (rgb / nir\_rgb / 4band)}
\label{sec:farslip_f4}

Three band-selection variants were evaluated (US-035), implemented as a crop
\textit{transform} in \texttt{ml/farslip/bands.py}: \textit{rgb}
(B04-B03-B02, 3 channels), \textit{nir\_rgb} (B08-B04-B03 false color, 3
channels) and \textit{4band} (B02-B03-B04-B08 identity, 4 channels). The 4band
variant reuses the $3\rightarrow4$ adaptation of the \texttt{patch\_embed} with
NIR\,=\,mean(RGB) (US-034) to avoid dead neurons when widening the first
convolutional block.

\paragraph{Evaluation caveat (honest partial result).}
The three runs exist and converge in distillation loss
(Table~\ref{tab:band_ablation}): the final total loss is very similar between
\textit{rgb} ($2.2893$) and \textit{nir\_rgb} ($2.2858$), and somewhat higher in
\textit{4band} ($2.3553$), with a notably higher \textit{patch} loss in 4band
($0.0786$ versus $\approx0.018$) consistent with the additional NIR channel.
However, the logs do not record the consolidated evaluation metric
(macro-F1/mIoU) per variant; we therefore do NOT report a classification winner
across bands. Reporting a non-existent F1 figure would be fabricating data.
Closing this table with the evaluation metric remains a pending task documented
in the epic \textit{blockers}.

\subsection{F5 --- Incremental 4$\rightarrow$18 Protocol and Ensemble Fusion}
\label{sec:farslip_f5}

The FarSLIP classifier is built incrementally
(\texttt{ml/farslip/incremental\_curriculum.py}): it starts from the 4 most
cardinal classes (Stage-1) and adds 2 classes per step up to 18 (Stage-2),
initializing each stage from the previous one with \texttt{strict=False} to allow
the head dimension to change. The stopping criterion (\texttt{stop\_criterion})
considers three explicit reasons: \textit{(i)} unacceptable new classes
(F1$<0.30$), \textit{(ii)} degradation of previous classes
($\text{drop}>0.05$, catastrophic forgetting), \textit{(iii)} maximum number of
classes reached.

Table~\ref{tab:cardinality_sweep} shows the per-parcel cardinality sweep --- the
empirical evidence that motivates the protocol. Difficulty grows monotonically
with the number of classes: macro-F1 $0.7025$ with 4 classes, $0.4579$ with 6,
$0.4075$ with 8, $0.3589$ with 10 and $0.3328$ with 12. The number of evaluable
parcels grows (from 1301 to 3200) as rarer classes are admitted, but the average
quality falls: minority classes with ambiguous phenology drag the macro-F1 down.

\input{../tables/farslip-method/cardinality_sweep.tex}

\paragraph{Honest 18-class-from-scratch fallback.}
The faithful 18-class baseline without class weights (variant
\texttt{faithful\_v2}, \textit{region\_category} MPCL + $L_{glo}$) reaches
macro-F1 $0.164$ and mIoU $0.111$, with 4 clear-signal classes
(Meadow $0.761$, Orchard $0.516$, Grapevine $0.489$, Corn $0.464$) and 14 classes
at zero or very low. The variant with \textit{inverse-frequency} class weights
(\texttt{faithful\_v2\_cw}) WORSENS the net balance to macro-F1 $0.102$: it
deflates the dominant classes more than it rescues the rare ones. These results
are reported without embellishment; the 18 classes are $\approx4.5\times$ harder
than the 4 easy ones ($0.576$ in US-036-a, not directly comparable).

\paragraph{Fusion in the heterogeneous ensemble.}
FarSLIP's net contribution is measured in the \textit{stacking} meta-model. The
5-member ensemble (TSViT-pheno, U-TAE, XGB-AlphaEarth, FarSLIP-ft18,
FarSLIP-zeroshot) over \textit{out-of-fold} probabilities reaches macro-F1
$0.6477$ (\textit{accuracy} $0.7935$) versus $0.6359$ for the 3-member ensemble
without FarSLIP: a delta of $+0.0118$ attributable to the two FarSLIP branches in
the OOF \textit{stacking}. The main manuscript reports the figure of the final
consolidated ensemble (macro-F1 $0.749$); the exact magnitude of the improvement
attributable to FarSLIP in that final figure differs from this OOF delta and is
treated as an editorial decision (see \textit{blockers}). In any case, the
conclusion is stable: FarSLIP's contribution is \textbf{positive but small}
($\approx+0.3\%$ to $+1.2\%$ depending on the metric), never the engine of the
classifier.

\subsection{\textit{Be My Eyes} Framework: The LLM Does Not Classify Pixels}
\label{sec:farslip_bemyeyes}

The result that text phenological prototypes contribute only $\approx0.3\%$ as a
supervised classifier --- and not the $5\%$ an optimistic reading would suggest
--- is an \textit{expected and correctly framed} result, not a failure. Under the
\textit{Be My Eyes} pattern of Huang et al.~\cite{huang2025bemyeyes}, the system
separates \textit{perceiver} and \textit{reasoner}: the \textit{perceivers} are
our dense models (TSViT-pheno, U-TAE) and FarSLIP, which \textit{see} each parcel
and emit observations; the \textit{reasoner} is a \textit{frozen} frontier LLM
(Gemini~3.5~Flash in the cloud, or Qwen3-30B-A3B on-prem for data sovereignty)
that reasons over those observations in plain text. The LLM \textbf{is not a
pixel classifier}; the final classifier is the heterogeneous ensemble.

Therefore, FarSLIP's phenological description should not be judged as a supervised
classification head (where its contribution is marginal), but as a
\textit{grounding signal} that enriches the \textit{reasoner}'s explanation
without sending tensors or logits to the LLM and without adding perceptible
latency. This separation explains why the negative result of F2 as a classifier
is compatible with FarSLIP's positive value as the copilot's vision-language
component.

\subsection{Explicit Caveats}
\label{sec:farslip_caveats}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Crude reprojection.} The orthogonal $384\rightarrow768$ lift is
    a linear mapping that preserves norm and angles, but it is \textit{not} the
    native \texttt{CLIPTextModel}: it is an approximation (caveat
    \texttt{ortho\_proj\_crude\_approx} recorded in the MLflow parameters). The
    path with the native text encoder remains future work.
    \item \textbf{Sample size.} The FarSLIP-vs-AlphaEarth evaluation is performed
    over $n=567$ parcels (order $\approx500$--$700$ per fold), with spatial
    cross-validation. The figures carry their standard deviation.
    \item \textbf{Parcel purity.} The \texttt{dominance\_ratio} filter and the
    parcel-level aggregation maintain high purity ($\approx98\%$) after
    discarding the patches over-dominated by Meadow; the residual mixing is
    absorbed into the dominant-parcel label.
    \item \textbf{Benchmark saturation.} Faithful FarSLIP does not beat AlphaEarth
    as a dense classifier ($-0.0024$); its value is complementary (ensemble) and
    in transferability, consistent with the limited spatial transferability
    reported for AlphaEarth-type embeddings.
\end{itemize}
